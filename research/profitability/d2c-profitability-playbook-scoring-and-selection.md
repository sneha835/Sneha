# D2C Profitability Playbook — Scoring &amp; Selection (Phase 4)

## Scoring method

Each of the 76 Phase 1 candidates was scored 0–3 on each of the 10 criteria specified in the brief (0=none/absent, 1=weak, 2=moderate, 3=strong), for a maximum composite score of 30:

1. Quality of profitability evidence (filed/audited &gt; company-reported &gt; third-party estimate &gt; unverified claim)
2. Availability of financial information (depth/multi-year trend, not just one data point)
3. Availability of strategic information (is there an actual pivot/decision/story, not just numbers)
4. Availability of founder/management interviews or direct quotes
5. Availability of marketing/campaign information
6. Availability of customer/channel information (retention, channel mix, D2C vs. retail split)
7. Relevance to D2C (how core the D2C model is vs. marketplace/wholesale-heavy)
8. Relevance to BabyOrgano (category adjacency — baby/kids/wellness/Ayurveda — or comparable stage/model)
9. Value as a comparison/control case (pairs well against another candidate, or fills a distinct failure/success mechanism not otherwise covered)
10. Geographic diversity (contribution to balanced India/US/UK/Europe/Australia coverage)

**Important methodological note on the final selection:** scoring within each region initially clustered UK candidates at the top (Companies House disclosure produces much stronger, filing-backed evidence than the mostly founder/agency-claimed figures found for German/French/Australian candidates this pass — itself a genuine Phase 1 finding, not an artifact). Strictly following composite-score rank within a fixed per-region quota would have produced zero non-UK European/Australian representation, contradicting the brief's explicit ask for UK/US/Europe/Australia coverage and criterion 10. The final selection below therefore deliberately trades one UK slot each for Adore Beauty (Australia) and About You (Germany) — both still strong, primary-filing-backed candidates (composite 20 each) — rather than mechanically taking the top 7 UK scores. This override is stated explicitly rather than silently applied.

## Why each was selected

1. **Good Glamm Group / The Moms Co.** — The single best-evidenced distress case in the whole universe (a named CEO publicly explaining the failure, NCLT court-adjacent records, multiple independent press confirmations), and it contains a baby/mom-care brand (The Moms Co.) directly inside the failure — lets the deep dive examine both the group-level roll-up failure mechanism and what happened to a BabyOrgano-adjacent brand caught inside it.
2. **FirstCry** — India's baby-category leader, now public, still loss-making at real scale (₹7,810 Cr revenue) — the clearest test of "does scale ever arrive at profitability, and does the market still reward an IPO without it."
3. **HealthKart** — The cleanest, most consistently multi-sourced profitability inflection found anywhere in this screening (loss → profit → tripled profit in two years) — a wellness-adjacent business to reverse-engineer for "what actually changed."
4. **Mamaearth/Honasa** — The most publicly documented Indian D2C company (post-IPO disclosure), and its profit *reversal* in FY25 (after achieving profit in FY24) makes it a rare case where a transition can be tested for durability, not just achievement.
5. **Kapiva** — The single closest category comparable to BabyOrgano found in the entire 76-company universe (Ayurveda-branded nutraceuticals, still pre-profitability) — most directly transferable lessons and most directly analogous risk profile.
6. **Hims &amp; Hers** — SEC-filed, unambiguous net-loss-to-net-income transition, with the company itself attributing it to subscription retention rather than paid-media efficiency — a primary-source-backed test of the Meta-diversification thesis.
7. **e.l.f. Beauty** — A profitable, SEC-filed beauty company built on mass retail and organic TikTok virality rather than a DTC-first, paid-social-first model — the strongest available counter-example to any "DTC + Meta" default assumption.
8. **Warby Parker** — SEC-filed; illustrates that even a "successful," well-known DTC brand can take 15 years and 4 years post-IPO to record its first full year of net income — a critical check against overstating how fast profitability inflections happen.
9. **Vital Farms** — SEC-filed "cleanest scale-up turnaround" in an independent analyst's public-company sample; useful as a clean financial-forensics exercise (operating margin 0%→11.6% over 4 years) in a non-beauty, non-fashion category.
10. **Morphe / Forma Brands** — Court-filed bankruptcy directly attributed by reporting to over-dependence on two influencer partnerships — the single clearest evidenced case anywhere in the universe of a creator/influencer strategy actively causing failure, which is directly relevant to the lab's Meta-diversification research (creators are often proposed as the alternative to Meta; this is a caution).
11. **Bobbie** — Direct infant-nutrition category adjacency to BabyOrgano, plus a distinctive vertical-integration move (acquiring a formula manufacturer) as part of its path to profitability.
12. **Gymshark** — Companies House-filed; the clearest available case of profit falling for three straight years *despite* revenue growth, explicitly explained by the company as deliberate reinvestment — a direct test of whether "profitable" is even the right question versus deliberate reinvestment choices.
13. **Bloom &amp; Wild** — The single most explicit, self-declared "we pivoted from growth to profitability, and here's what we cut" statement found in the entire universe — the textbook Phase 5 case study.
14. **JoJo Maman Bébé** — Companies House-filed; decades of modest, sustained profitability in the baby category via omnichannel (stores + catalog + online) rather than a paid-social-DTC model — the strongest available "boring but durable profitability" baby-category comparator for BabyOrgano.
15. **Made.com** — The best-evidenced case in the universe of a D2C failure caused by something other than marketing or CAC (a 5.5x freight-cost spike) — essential for the Phase 5/6 instruction to actively search for contradictory evidence rather than assuming Meta/CAC explains everything.
16. **Mamas &amp; Papas** — Companies House-filed baby-category failure (administration), which — paired directly against JoJo Maman Bébé in the same category and country — gives the lab a clean, matched success/failure comparison exactly as CLAUDE.md's "repeatable patterns... category-specific differences" principle asks for.
17. **Adore Beauty** — ASX-filed; a clean instance where underlying operating profit (EBIT +74.8%) and statutory/headline profit (down 65%) moved in opposite directions in the same year — an essential financial-forensics teaching case, and gives Australia real representation in the deep-dive set.
18. **About You** — Publicly filed (Frankfurt); a €2bn-revenue business still posting a nine-figure net loss, now being absorbed by a direct competitor (Zalando) — the clearest European parallel to India's "scale without profit, eventually consolidates" pattern (Good Glamm, Purplle), and gives continental Europe real representation.
